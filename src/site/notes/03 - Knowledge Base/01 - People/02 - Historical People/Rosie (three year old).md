# Rosie
**Type**: Spectral Apparition / Child Ghost
**Aliases**: Jeanette Roseanne "Rosie" Greger, Little Rosie

**First mentioned**: [[02 - Video GSNC/Volume I (2021-2022)/01 - July 2021 - September 2021/2021-08-01 - Shanley Hotel\|2021-08-01 - Shanley Hotel]]
**Locations**: [[03 - Knowledge Base/03 - Locations/The Shanley Hotel\|The Shanley Hotel]] (Napanoch, New York), Specifically "Rosie's Room" in the second-floor bordello wing, the Hoornbeek Farm well area (across the street).

## Description:
Rosie is the tragic spirit of a three-year-old girl who lived briefly at the hotel during the early 20th century. Unlike the adult spirits of the bordello wing, Rosie is celebrated as a sweet, innocent, yet highly interactive child entity. In paranormal pop culture, she is recognized as one of the hotel's anchor spirits, commonly associated with the sound of phantom giggling and moving toys. 

## Associated Events:
* **May 26, 1911 (The Tragedy):** While playing outdoors near the hotel, three-year-old Rosie lifted a wooden board covering a deep water well, lost her balance, and tragically fell to her death from a severe head injury and drowning. 
* **2005–Present (The Toy Offering Sanctuary):** Upon the hotel's restoration and opening to ghost hunting tourism, Rosie's designated bedroom was transformed into a shrine filled with dolls, stuffed animals, and interactive toys left by travelers.
* **2026 (The Toy Piano Capture):** Paranormal teams testing children's trigger items documented an Electronic Voice Phenomenon (EVP) event where a toy piano was manipulated and a soft female voice murmured "thank you".

## Statements/Claims:
* **The Barber's Daughter Evidence:** Historical record trackers confirmed that Rosie was not a Shanley family child, but rather the daughter of Peter Greger, the hotel’s resident barber who moved his family away immediately following the heartbreak.
* **The "Bedtime Story" Reports:** Overnight guests regularly file written claims in hotel logs stating they felt a small presence tugging at their blankets or blankets shifting, with some claiming the activity ceases only if they read a children's storybook aloud to the empty room.
* **The Playroom Audio Claims:** Audio researchers routinely log captures of disembodied, pitch-perfect childlike laughter echoing through the second-floor hallway during peak night hours.

## Behavior/Characteristics:
* **Playful Interaction:** She is known to express her presence by activating kinetic light-up toys, rolling small balls across the floor, or mimicking tapping sounds.
* **Gentle/Innocent Energy:** Unlike the heavy, dark energies described on the hotel's third floor, Rosie’s presence is reported as comforting, warm, and highly receptive to maternal energy.

## Connections:
* **Peter Greger:** Her historical father and the local Napanoch barber.
* **[[03 - Knowledge Base/01 - People/02 - Historical People/Maddie (Lady of the evening)\|Maddie]] and [[03 - Knowledge Base/01 - People/02 - Historical People/Anna (Lady of the evening)\|Anna]]:** The adult bordello spirits who share the second floor and are colloquially rumored by investigators to "babysit" or shield Rosie's spirit.
* **The Shanley Children:** Kathleen, James Jr., and William—Beatrice Shanley's three infant children who also died at the hotel and are thought to join Rosie in spectral play.

## Patterns:
* **Trigger Object Responsiveness:** Rosie follows a standard child-ghost pattern where activity spikes dramatically when specialized childhood objects (like light-up cat balls, music boxes, or porcelain dolls) are introduced to the space.
* **Coexistence with Adult Entities:** Her haunting signature is deeply layered with the surrounding bordello spirits, leading to overlapping EVPs where adult female voices interact alongside child-sized responses.

## Uncertainty:
* **Location Disconnect:** Historical data locates the actual well drowning on the Hoornbeek Farm property *across the street* rather than directly inside the hotel. It is uncertain whether her spirit is tied to her actual place of death or if she returned to the hotel because it was her primary place of comfort and life.
* **Identity Blending:** Because the Shanley family lost three infants on the property, skeptics argue that it is impossible to verify whether the child-centric poltergeist activity belongs uniquely to Rosie or if it is a blend of residual echoes from the other deceased children.
