# Wangi Wangi Power Station
**Location**: Wangi Point Road, Wangi Wangi, City of Lake Macquarie, New South Wales
**Country**: Australia
**Type**: Abandoned Coal-Fired Power Station / Industrial Ruins
**Investigated by**: [[Sam and Colby\|Sam and Colby]], The Ghost Gals, independent urban exploration (Urbex) teams, and regional paranormal digital creators.
## History:
- #### Post-War Construction (1949–1958): 
	- Commissioned during severe post-war energy rationing. Over 1,000 workers, including post-war Italian immigrants, lived in a sprawling tent city on the shore of Lake Macquarie to construct the facility. 
- #### The Architectural Legacy: 
	- Designed by project architect Colin Smith of C.H. Smith & Johnson. Built eleven storeys high in massive triple-brick over a riveted steel frame, it remains a rare, beautiful example of the classic English brick-massing industrial tradition.
- #### Grid Supremacy: 
	- At its operational peak, it generated 330 megawatts of electricity, supplying up to one-fifth of the entire power grid of New South Wales. It famously saved the state during the total grid blackout of June 10, 1964.
- #### Decommissioning (1986):
	- Closed permanently after newer, more cost-efficient local facilities made it redundant. The generating turbines and heavy internal infrastructure were stripped between 1995 and 1997, leaving the shell empty. It was added to the NSW State Heritage Register in 1999.
## Alleged Activity:
- #### Massive EMF Residuals:
	- Due to decades of generating massive high-voltage electricity, investigators state the leftover energy field causes constant, erratic spikes on electromagnetic field (EMF) meters.
- #### The Stalking Shadows: 
	- Numerous accounts of solid, pitch-black shadow figures seen darting behind the massive concrete boiler structural pillars or standing on upper catwalks.
- #### Auditory Disembodied Footsteps: 
	- Heavy boot steps matching old industrial workers echoing across concrete floors when the structural halls are entirely locked down or vacant.
- #### Spatial Chills: 
	- Reports of intense, localized freezing zones manifesting rapidly inside the dark basement machinery areas.

## Entities/Spirits:
- #### [[Irene McColl\|Irene McColl]]: 
	- A tragic historical local fixture. In the summer of 1952, the body of the three-year-old girl was discovered inside a rainwater-filled septic tank on the property boundary after a severe thunderstorm. Her spirit is said to trigger childish whispers and running footsteps.
- #### The Three Workers: 
	- Residual entities matching the three legally recorded personnel who tragically suffered fatal accidents or machinery crushing injuries during its 30 operational years.
- #### Catwalk Watcher: 
	- An aggressive, territorial male presence that patrols the high steel structural frames and low-growls at trespassers.
## Important Events:
- #### 1952 - The McColl Tragedy:
	- The accidental drowning of young Irene on the industrial perimeter, anchoring the site's initial tragic lore.
- #### June 10, 1964 - Black Start Savior:
	- The NSW grid went entirely dark; the station's stoker-fired boilers successfully brought the entire state back online.
- #### 1999 - Heritage Shield: 
	- Officially recognized as an untouchable monument on the NSW State Heritage Register, saving the colossal ruins from impending corporate demolition.
## Recurring Elements:
- #### "The Pommy Design" Heat Loop:
	- The original structure was a British design built to heavily trap internal heat, a disastrous feature for the blistering Australian sun that required workers to constantly consume salt tablets to survive. Modern explorers report localized, heavy heat pressure drops in sealed rooms.
- #### Open Floor Shaft Hazards: 
	- The building features hidden, decaying floor drop-aways and open holes leading multiple storeys straight down into pitch-black subterranean water crevices.

	- The 22.3-hectare coastal complex has been repeatedly targeted for major residential, marina, and commercial tourism redevelopments since the late 1990s, though no project has ever successfully broken ground.
