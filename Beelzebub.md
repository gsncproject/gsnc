# Beelzebub
**Type**: Demonized Deity / Prince of Demons / Synonym for Satan
**Aliases**: Beelzebul, Baal-Zebub, Lord of the Flies, Lord of Dung/Filth, Prince of Demons, First King of Hell

**First mentioned**: c. 1400 BCE (Ugaritic texts as Baal); 9th Century BCE (Hebrew Bible: 2 Kings 1:2–3), [[2021-07-25 - Conjuring House]]
**Locations**: Ekron (Philistine city), Canaan, The Underworld/Hell
## Description:
An ancient Semitic and Philistine deity who was progressively transformed over millennia into one of Christianity's primary demonic figures. Originally revered as a storm, fertility, or high lord ("Baal Zebul"), Jewish and Christian theological polemics reinterpreted his name to mean "Lord of the Flies" or "Lord of Dung" to mock the foreign idol. In classic occult demonology, he is illustrated as a grotesque giant fly or a multi-headed beast.
## Associated Events:
* **9th Century BCE (The Injury of Ahaziah)**: King Ahaziah of Israel fell through his upper chamber and sent messengers to consult the oracle of Baal-Zebub in Ekron, sparking the wrath of the prophet Elijah.
* **c. 30 CE (The Synoptic Accusations)**: Religious leaders accused Jesus of Nazareth of performing exorcisms using the dark authority of Beelzebub rather than the Spirit of God.
* **16th–17th Century (Grimoire Era)**: Demonologists formalized occult hierarchies, codifying Beelzebub as a supreme Prince of Hell and commander of the Order of the Fly.

## Statements/Claims:
* **The Pharisees' Accusation**: "It is only by Beelzebul, the prince of demons, that this man drives out demons." (Matthew 12:24).
* **Jesus' Response**: Argument of logic stating that a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand, claiming that if Satan/Beelzebub casts out Satan, his rule would collapse (Matthew 12:25–26).
* **Testament of Solomon Claim**: An early apocryphal text claims Beelzebub was originally a high-ranking heavenly angel associated with the evening star Hesperus before his fall.
## Behavior/Characteristics:
* **Theological Role**: Associated heavily with the deadly sin of Gluttony, false gods, idolatry, and the dominion over unclean spirits.
* **Attributes**: Possesses the power of flight, commands localized swarms of insects, and is characterized by decay, filth, and spiritual corruption.
* **Literary Persona**: Depicted in John Milton’s *Paradise Lost* as a majestic, deeply intelligent, and solemn captain of Hell who ranks second only to Satan himself.
## Connections:
* **Baal**: The primary Canaanite storm god from which his identity was fractured and demonized.
* **Satan / Lucifer**: Often conflated with or serving directly beneath him as the co-ruler of the demonic host.
* **Ahaziah**: The Israelite king whose reliance on Beelzebub's oracle sealed his biblical downfall.
* **Jesus Christ**: The historical figure who directly confronted accusations of acting as Beelzebub's agent.
## Patterns:
* **Linguistic Demonization**: A repeating historical pattern where winning religious factions intentionally warp the names of conquered deities (e.g., turning "Exalted Lord" into "Lord of Dung") to discredit rival belief systems.
* **Scapegoating of Exorcists**: Mirroring the accusations against Jesus, historical "miracle workers" and healers throughout the Middle Ages were routinely accused of negotiating with Beelzebub to control lesser spirits.
## Uncertainty:
* **Linguistic Intent**: Scholars debate whether "Baal-Zebub" was his actual native name in Ekron (implying a god who controlled fly plagues) or if it was strictly an insulting Israelite pun meant to degrade "Baal-Zebul" (Lord of the High House).
* **Hierarchical Separation**: In mainstream Christian theology, Beelzebub is treated as another name for Satan; however, in precise occultism and grimoires, they are distinctly separate entities with completely different origins.
